Case study 07 · Identity & product · 2025

Kiln, a studio you can carry.

A shared ceramics studio with three locations, one wall-mounted whiteboard and a booking system held together by a group chat. We gave it an identity, a booking app and a way to tell members their work had come out of the kiln.

The brief

Make the admin disappear.

Research · 24 members 2 studio residencies

Kiln's members were not asking for an app. They were asking to stop losing pieces. A bisque load takes eleven hours and finishes at three in the morning; by the time anyone posts a photo of the shelf, half the work has been claimed by the wrong people and the other half has been quietly moved to make room.

We spent two weeks in the Sheffield studio, then two more in Ghent, mostly holding a clipboard near the kiln. The insight was not about scheduling. It was that the studio already had a perfectly good system — a numbered shelf, a chalk board and a habit of asking the person next to you — and every digital tool they had tried replaced that system instead of reading it.

So the app photographs the shelf. It knows which firing your pieces went into, it tells you the moment the kiln drops below 200°C, and it does nothing else at all until you ask it to.

What changed

Twelve weeks after launch.

94% of members opted into firing notifications in the first fortnight
−71% messages in the studio group chat, measured against the same twelve weeks last year
3 min median time from kiln cool-down to a member knowing their work is ready
“It reads the studio instead of arguing with it. Nobody has touched the whiteboard since May.”
Ines Duarte Founder, Kiln Ceramics Collective
