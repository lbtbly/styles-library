Letter from the land manager

We measured less than we hoped, and published it anyway.

The peat at Corrie Dubh took 14 months longer to wet up than the model said it would. That single delay accounts for most of the −6.1% gap between what we forecast in 2025 and what we can defend today. We have left the original forecast in the archive rather than quietly restating it.

Across the seventeen sites we planted 38,900 broadleaves, blocked 11.2 km of drainage grip, and removed 2.4 km of deer fence that had outlived its purpose. Every figure in this report is traceable to a survey line you can walk yourself; the coordinates are in appendix C.

Nothing here is offset accounting. We do not sell the carbon we retire, and we do not count a hectare twice.

Method

How a hectare gets counted.

01 — Walked

Fixed-point transects

Every site is walked on a 20 m grid twice a year, in April and October, by two surveyors working independently.

02 — Sampled

Cores, not estimates

Peat depth comes from physical cores at 1 in 40 grid points. Nothing is inferred from satellite alone.

03 — Published

Raw before rounded

The unrounded survey file ships with the report. Where the two surveyors disagree by more than 5%, both numbers stay in.
