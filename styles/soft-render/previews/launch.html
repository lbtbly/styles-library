Three things, done properly.

We cut everything that made the last one clever and kept only what made it useful.

Light that arrives early

A 27-minute ramp from 1800K to 3400K, driven by a 12-bit dimmer so the first steps are genuinely invisible. It reaches full warmth two minutes before the tone starts.

Sound with a floor, not a ceiling

A 62 mm driver in a sealed birch chamber. Pink noise, rain on canvas, and a single low room tone recorded at Hoxa Sound in Orkney. Nothing streams; everything is on the device.

Nothing to sign into

Set it with the dial on the top, or with the companion app over Bluetooth once and never again. No account, no telemetry, no firmware that phones home at 4am.

How a night goes.

01

You turn the dial

One ring, one press. The ring reads the room's temperature and light level and picks a tone; you can override it, but after the first week most people stop.

02

The room settles

Output drops on a 40-minute curve. If the room warms past 21°C, the tone thins out slightly — the only thing Coda ever does without being asked.

03

Light comes back

The ramp starts 27 minutes before your wake time. Sound joins in the last four minutes at 18 dB. There is no snooze, and there is no second alarm.
